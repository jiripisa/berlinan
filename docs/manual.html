Getting started

Berlinan is a generative MIDI sequencer for macOS 14 or later. It runs a small ensemble of sequencer voices that repeat, drift out of phase and earn their changes over time. It makes no sound itself: it sends MIDI to Ableton Live or to hardware synths.

  1. Download the disk image, open it and drag Berlinan to Applications. The app is signed and notarized, so it opens without security warnings.
  2. Start Berlinan. It opens with the Demo trio (Pluck 1, Pluck 2 and Bass) in A minor, from a fresh random seed every session.
  3. Press Space. The ensemble starts playing.
  4. Send it somewhere you can hear it. Pick the MIDI output and set up your synths as described in Connecting synths.
  5. Play with it. Press 1 to 7 to move the whole ensemble to another scale degree, N for a whole new musical universe, and watch the horizon strip announce what is about to change.

Every change you make lands on the next bar line, never in the middle of one. That is deliberate: it is how the genre works, and it means you can press buttons early and in time.

The main window

The window is built in horizontal bands, top to bottom.

1Transport bar
Play/stop, position, tempo, key and degree; new seed, templates, settings, clock source and MIDI output.
2Harmony strip
How the harmony moves and the next harmonic changes.
3Horizon strip
A plain-language list of everything that is about to happen.
4Voice lanes
One lane per voice, with its pattern, playhead and controls. Scroll when there are more voices than fit.
5Performance panel
The jam controls: degrees, snapshots, B-part, arranger, density, MIDI learn and export.
6Convergence footer
When all loops line up again, and the engine version.

Hold the pointer over any control to see a short tooltip.

Transport bar

Play and readouts

1Play / Stop Space
Starts and stops the ensemble. Stopping silences every note at once, including long drones. Every play starts the piece from its beginning; each play-to-stop pass is recorded as a take.
2POS
The position as bar.beat while playing, –.– when stopped.
3BPM
The tempo. Change it in Project settings; in EXT or LINK mode it follows the external clock.
4KEY
The root and scale every voice shares, for example A naturalMinor.
5DEG
The current transposition: 1 is home, +3 means the ensemble plays three scale degrees up. It turns orange when you are away from home.

New seed, templates, settings, sync and output

1New seed N
A whole new musical universe from the same setup: new patterns for every voice. While playing it takes over at the next bar. ⌘Z brings the previous universe back.
2Templates
Starting points. See Templates.
3Project settings
Key, scale, tempo, meter and slides. See Project settings.
4Clock source
INT, EXT or LINK. See Clock sync.
5MIDI output
Where the notes go. See MIDI output.

Templates

Click the grid icon to start from a template. Each one starts from a fresh seed, so you never get the same piece twice.

  • Demo trio: two plucks of 16 and 15 steps over a 16-step bass. The default when the app starts.
  • Full ensemble: six voices on channels 1 to 5 and 10: two plucks, a bass-melody walk, a chord-layer pad, a lead ready for melody promotion and a heartbeat kick.
  • Ambient drift: drone-led, about one change a minute, with the arranger switched on and two CC scenes (closed and open) for a filter journey.

The built-in templates start in A minor. Templates you saved yourself appear under My templates. Save as Template… stores the current setup there (see Projects and templates).

Picked while playing, a template takes over at the next bar without stopping the clock; the horizon shows TEMPLATE … · next bar until then.

Project settings

Click the gear to open the sheet, change values and press Apply.

1Root and Mode
The key. Modes: natural minor, dorian, phrygian, mixolydian, lydian, lydian dominant, harmonic minor, minor pentatonic, locrian and major.
2BPM
40 to 220 beats per minute.
3Meter
4/4, 3/4, 6/8, 7/8 (4+3 or 3+4), 5/4 (3+2) and 5/8 (3+2).
4Slides
Lets mutations tie bass and lead steps into glides. Set those synths to mono with portamento of about 60 to 120 ms.

While playing, key and tempo changes land on the next bar; meter and slides need the transport stopped. Applied while stopped, the patterns regenerate in the new key or meter from the same seeds, so the material stays recognizable.

Clock sync

  • INT: Berlinan is the clock master and sends MIDI clock and Start/Stop to its output, so arpeggiators and sequencers in your synths follow.
  • EXT: Berlinan follows incoming MIDI clock. Start and Stop from the master start and stop Berlinan, and the tempo follows. The status reads EXT — waiting for clock until Start arrives.
  • LINK: Berlinan joins the Ableton Link session on your network. Tempo and bar phase lock both ways; the status shows the number of peers.

The clock source can only be changed while stopped. Setup details are in Connecting synths.

MIDI output

Click the output name at the right end of the transport bar (Berlinan Out by default) and choose:

  • Berlinan Out (virtual): a virtual MIDI port that Ableton Live and other apps see as an input.
  • A connected device under Devices: a USB synth, a DIN MIDI interface or an IAC bus. Notes, CC and clock go straight to it.

The choice is remembered. You can switch while playing; whatever still sounds on the previous output is silenced first. The dot next to the name is green when the output is reachable and red when the chosen device is unplugged (the name then reads disconnected); sending resumes when you plug it back in.

Harmony strip

The strip shows how the harmony moves. Click the mode name (here manual schedule) to pick another mode; this works while stopped.

  • Manual schedule (demo walk): a fixed walk of transpositions written into the project.
  • Progression: a looped chord walk of four bars per step: i-iv-v-i, 1-7-6-5, 1-5-6-7, 1-6-4-7, 1-6-4-1, 7-5-6, 1-b7-6-#5 or 6-5-4-1.
  • Weighted random (sweet/dark): a surprise move every 8 to 16 bars, drawn from the genre's sweet and dark targets.
  • Bass reharmonization: the upper voices stay where they are and only the bass moves through the chosen progression.

Next to the mode, cyan text counts down to the next change (→ +5 in 5 bars). The chips on the right list the next four changes as degree@bar: +3@13 means three degrees up at bar 13, 1@17 back home at bar 17.

Horizon strip

The horizon strip tells you, in words, what is about to happen, so nothing surprises you mid-set. Each chip is one upcoming event:

1Arranger
The arranger's current phase and how far away its next change is (BUILD · change in ~11 bars).
2Next mutation
The voice whose pattern will be allowed to mutate soonest (Drone may mutate · ~128 bars).

Other chips appear for anything you have queued: a key change, a snapshot recall, density, the B-part, a voice switched on or off, alternation, a tension stab, a melody promoted to the lead, an arranger advance, a scene morph or a template load. Each says next bar or in N bars. With nothing scheduled the strip reads steady — nothing scheduled.

Voice lanes

Each voice has a lane: its name and channel on the left, the pattern in the middle and its buttons on the right.

Name and MIDI channel

1Name
The voice by its role: Pluck 1, Pluck 2, Bass, Pad, Lead, Drone, Heartbeat. A role used twice is numbered.
2MIDI channel
Click ch N to send the voice on another channel, 1 to 16. A channel already used by another voice shows that voice's name. Works while playing; the change is saved with the project.
3Loop length
The pattern's length in sixteenth steps. Different lengths are what make the voices drift against each other.
4Lifecycle dots
How many more times the pattern plays unchanged before it may mutate. Filled dots are plays left; all empty with a cyan ring means the pattern is established and may change. Amber dots mean the pattern is locked.

Pattern strip

The strip shows the voice's current pattern on a sixteenth grid with stronger lines on the beats. Higher notes sit higher. Accented notes have an orange outline. The orange line is the voice's own playhead: since loops differ in length, the playheads of the lanes visibly drift apart.

  • Drag the strip into an Ableton Live clip slot to get the current pattern as a MIDI clip, looped to a whole number of bars (up to 16).
  • The strip dims when the voice is muted or held back by the arranger, and darkens for a blank cycle.
  • A WAITS badge means the arranger is holding the voice back; it joins on a later bar.

Lane buttons

1Mute
Silences the voice at once. The engine keeps running and the take keeps recording it, so unmuting brings it back in place.
2Lock
Freezes the pattern: it keeps repeating and never mutates.
3Regenerate
A new pattern for this voice at its next loop. A bass-melody voice gets a new walk over the same harmony. The drone and heartbeat are fixed and have nothing to regenerate. ⌘Z undoes it.
4Blank cycle
The voice sits out one loop, then comes back. A classic way to make room.
5Edit
Opens the pattern editor. The button stays amber while the voice plays a hand-edited pattern.
6MIDI delay
Plays the voice's echoes as real MIDI notes, for setups without an audio delay. Turn the audio delay down on that channel when it is on (the Delay Cheat Sheet warns you).
7Melody extraction
Mutes the voice except for three or four accented notes, which become a melody that evolves every few bars. Turn it off to get the full pattern back.

Lane menu

Right-click a pattern strip for more:

  • CC Profile: map the voice's modulation lanes to your synth's controllers (Generic, Microfreak, or your own profiles).
  • Promote Melody to Lead: appears while melody extraction is on and the project has a lead voice; hands the extracted line to the lead at the next bar.
  • Alternate With: this voice and another play in alternating four-bar phrases, call and answer. Stop Alternation ends it.
  • Voice Off / Voice On (engine): switches the voice out of (or into) the ensemble at the next bar; the arranger uses the same switch.

Pattern editor

The pencil button opens a note table for the voice's pattern.

1Loop
The pattern length, 2 to 64 sixteenth steps.
2Notes
One row per note: the step it starts on, its scale degree (0 is the root, 7 an octave up), an octave shift, the velocity and whether it is accented. The trash icon removes a note. Degrees follow the key and every transposition.
3Add note
Adds a note on the first free step.

Apply replaces the pattern and locks it, so it will not mutate. While playing, the new pattern starts at the voice's next loop. The edit is saved with the project.

Performance panel

The band under the lanes holds the controls you play during a set. Everything here lands on the next bar.

Degrees 1–7

1Degree buttons
Move every voice to scale degree 1 to 7 at the next bar. Because all voices share one scale, the whole ensemble changes harmony together. The active degree is lit; 1 is home.
2Snapshots
⌥-click a slot to store the complete current state (all patterns, lifecycles and harmony); filled slots turn cyan. Click a filled slot to recall it at the next bar; ⌘Z undoes a recall. Snapshots are saved with the project.

Re-roll, B-part and tension

1Re-roll unlocked
Regenerates every voice that is not locked, each at its next loop. ⌘Z undoes it.
2B
The B-part move, the genre's classic second section, in one press: a progression starts, the bass gets a new line and the bed drops out. Available while playing.
3T·n
The tension budget: n out-of-key moments left for this piece. Each press plays one sustained minor second against the root at the next bar. Used sparingly, it is the genre's spice.
4Scene
Only shown when the project has CC scenes (Ambient drift has two). Pick a scene and all mapped controllers glide toward that timbre over eight bars. Available while playing.

Arranger

1AUTO
Switches the arranger autopilot on or off. It builds the piece voice by voice (a drone opens it when there is one), lets it settle, varies it, fires the B-part, brings the theme back at the payoff, thins it out and ends on a roots-only bar. It only advises: every move it makes is also a control you can use yourself, and you can override it at any time.
2Phase
The current phase and how many bars it has lasted: BUILD, BED, VAR, B-PART, PAYOFF, SUBTR or END.
3Advance
Fires the arranger's next change at the next bar instead of waiting.
4Ambient mode
Slows everything down to about one change a minute; patterns play 16 times before they may mutate. Set it while stopped.
5DENS
Thins out or fills the whole mix: it caps how many notes each loop plays, and accents survive longest. The arranger raises density at the payoff and lowers it on the way out.

Right-click AUTO (while stopped) to choose a form: Classic arc (phases of 8 to 16 bars, with a reprise), Slow build (12 to 24 bars, with a reprise) or Ambient plateau (about one change a minute).

MIDI learn

Map your controller's knobs and pads to Berlinan and leave the laptop alone.

  1. Send your controller to the MIDI input Berlinan Control.
  2. Click LEARN 1. It reads LEARN: PICK A CONTROL.
  3. Click the control to map: play/stop, a degree button, new seed, re-roll, a snapshot slot, B or advance. It reads MOVE A KNOB….
  4. Move a knob or press a pad. The mapping is stored and survives restarts.
  5. Map more controls the same way, then click LEARN again to finish.

Only the press counts: a note-on, or a controller value of 64 or more, so a pad that sends a value on release does not trigger twice.

Export capture

Berlinan always records the last hour of what it played. EXPORT CAPTURE 2 saves the last 8, 16, 32 or 64 whole bars as a multitrack MIDI file, one track per voice, ready to drop into Live. Takes… opens the Takes window.

The capture records what the engine played, including muted voices, so a voice you muted for the room is still there in the file.

Convergence footer

LCM tells you after how many bars, and how many seconds, all loops line up again: the least common multiple of the loop lengths. Until then every bar is a combination you have not heard yet. The right end shows the engine version.

Takes window

Every play-to-stop pass is kept as a take, on disk, until you delete it. Open the window from Window ▸ Takes or Export Capture ▸ Takes…. The newest take is on top; the one being recorded shows a red dot and grows.

1Take
Click the name to rename it, then press Return. Below it: when it was recorded, its length in bars and minutes, the tempo and the voices. Drag a take into Live or a Finder folder to get it as a MIDI file.
2Save as .mid
Saves a multitrack MIDI file, one track per voice on its channel.
3Delete
Removes the take from disk after you confirm.

A template loaded while playing starts a new take at its bar line. The take in progress is saved every 10 seconds, so a crash costs you at most those seconds.

Delay Cheat Sheet

Delay is the genre's reverb. Window ▸ Delay Cheat Sheet lists, for every voice, the audio delay division and time at the current tempo: dotted eighths for plucks, eighths for the bass, quarters for sustained voices. The copy button puts the settings on the clipboard. Set feedback and wet to about 50 %, use ping-pong and cut lows and highs on the repeats.

When a lane's MIDI delay is on, the sheet warns you that the echoes are already in the MIDI.

Updates

When Berlinan starts, it checks whether a new version is out. If there is one, a window shows what is new, with Install Update, Remind Me Later and Skip This Version. Nothing is downloaded or installed until you click Install Update; Berlinan then downloads the new version, checks its signature, replaces itself and restarts.

1Check for updates when Berlinan starts
On by default. Open Berlinan ▸ Settings… ⌘, and untick it to stop the check.
2Check Now
Checks right away and also tells you when you are up to date. The same is Berlinan ▸ Check for Updates….

Connecting synths

Ableton Live

  1. In Berlinan, leave the output on Berlinan Out (virtual).
  2. In Live, create one MIDI track per voice with an instrument on it.
  3. Set each track's MIDI From to Berlinan Out and the channel to the voice's channel (the ch N label in its lane).
  4. Set Monitor to In (or arm the track) and press play in Berlinan.

To record, arm the tracks in Live, or simply export a take afterwards and drag it in.

Hardware synths

  1. Connect the synth over USB or a MIDI interface.
  2. Pick it in the MIDI output menu.
  3. Set the synth to receive on the channel of the voice it should play, or move the voice to the synth's channel with its channel menu. A multitimbral synth can play several voices, one per channel.

In INT mode Berlinan also sends MIDI clock, so the synth's arpeggiator or sequencer runs in time.

Following Live's tempo

  • Ableton Link (easiest): switch Berlinan to LINK and turn Link on in Live. Tempo and bar phase are shared both ways; start Berlinan with Space.
  • MIDI clock: switch Berlinan to EXT. In Live's MIDI settings, turn on Sync for an output port Berlinan can hear, such as the IAC Driver bus (enable it in Audio MIDI Setup). Starting Live then starts Berlinan.

Projects and templates

  • File ▸ Save Project As… ⇧⌘S saves a .berlinan file with the whole setup: voices, channels, key, tempo, harmony mode, hand-edited patterns, the seed and the snapshot slots.
  • File ▸ Open Project… ⌘O opens one (while stopped). A saved project keeps its own seed and key, so it comes back exactly as you left it. A damaged file is refused with an explanation instead of being loaded.
  • Templates ▸ Save as Template… stores the current setup as a template; it then appears under My templates. Unlike a project, a template always starts from a fresh seed.

Concepts

Repetition earns change
New material plays verbatim four times before it may mutate (sixteen in ambient mode), and each mutation has to earn its place again. The lifecycle dots show where each voice stands.
Degrees, not notes
Patterns are written in scale degrees. Transposing moves every voice by the same number of degrees within the scale, so harmony stays in key.
Phasing
Voices loop at different lengths and drift against each other until they line up again after the LCM shown in the footer.
Seed
A number that decides every random choice. The same project and seed always give the same music; a new seed gives a new piece from the same setup.
Next bar
Changes never land mid-bar. Pattern changes for a single voice wait for that voice's next loop.

Troubleshooting

I press play but hear nothing

Check the output: with Berlinan Out, a track in Live must listen to Berlinan Out on the voice's channel with monitoring on. With a device, the dot must be green and the synth must receive on the voice's channel. In EXT mode, Berlinan waits for Start from the clock master.

The output shows “disconnected”

The chosen device is unplugged or switched off. Plug it back in and sending resumes on its own, or pick another output.

Two voices sound on the same synth

They share a MIDI channel. Open a lane's channel menu: a channel used by another voice shows its name.

A menu or button is greyed out

Some settings only change while stopped: clock source, harmony mode, meter, slides, ambient mode and the arranger form. B, advance, tension and scenes work while playing.

A note keeps sounding

Press stop: Berlinan sends note-offs and All Notes Off on every channel it used.
